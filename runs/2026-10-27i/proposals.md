## 1. InkHeirloom — grandma's letters, as a book
- **Gap:** the options today are archival transcription tools ([Transkribus](https://www.transkribus.org/pricing), [Handwriting OCR](https://www.handwritingocr.com/personal-handwriting-ocr)) **or** hand-made binding services ([4everBound](https://4everbound.com/pages/legacypreserve): $449 deposit). Nothing does both: photos on your phone in, AI transcription, translation (old German, Italian or French letters) and historical context, then a printed book with the letter and its transcript side by side.
- **Money:** $129–199 per printed book through a print-on-demand API (Lulu or Blurb); digital edition $39. Peaks at the holidays and Mother's/Father's Day. Proven gift category (Storyworth).
- **Build:** 6–7 days. **Domains (unchecked):** inkheirloom.com · lettersbook.app · grandmasletters.com
- **Risks:** accuracy on hard handwriting (needs an easy correction screen); seasonal consumer marketing.

## 2. SparkPass — pass the electrician exam
- **Gap:** pass rates are brutal. About 28% of Texas journeyman electrician attempts pass ([TradesmanPass](https://insights.tradesmanpass.com/journeyman-electrician-exam-pass-rates-by-state-2026-statist/)), and the master calculations section is around 18–19% ([TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/agendas/2026-05-12-elc-staff-reports.pdf)). Exams are open-book (the National Electrical Code), so the skill is *finding the answer fast*. Prep today is printed study guides, code-book tab sets and practice-question apps.
- **Money:** $49–99 per exam cycle. Partner with trade schools and union apprenticeship programs.
- **Build:** 6 days. Licensing the code text is a hurdle: you can't reproduce NEC text freely, so the product would teach navigation by article numbers.
- **Risks:** a crowded practice-question market; rules on what you may bring into the exam differ by state.

## 3–4 (brief)

AI tools such as Granted AI exist, but none was found doing "AI draft plus SBIR reviewer sign-off" at about $1,500.
- **PanelPath:** credentialing costs $79–450 per insurer and takes 60–120 days, with heavy competition (Headway and Alma do it free in exchange for a cut of revenue).
